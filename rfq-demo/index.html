What it does

The supplier who sends an accurate quote first usually gets the order, and the first pass on every RFQ is the same work: cross-reference the part numbers, check the alternates, check stock and lead time, price it.

The system does that pass. It reads an inbound RFQ in whatever shape it arrives, matches the requested part numbers against your catalogue and approved alternates, pulls stock, lead time and pricing, and drafts a finished quote in your own name and in the same email thread, within a minute or two. Clear-cut RFQs can go out on their own if you allow it; anything uncertain is emailed to you first, with the finished quote attached and a link that sends it. Releasing one is a click in the mailbox you already have open, not a queue somebody has to remember to check. It runs overnight and at weekends, so replies keep going out around the clock.

On your systems, not ours

There is no platform to move onto and no licence to sign. It reads your data, in your formats, follows your quoting rules, and can run inside your own network, so your catalogue and pricing stay entirely within your systems and only the finished quote leaves. The build is yours.

Because the engine exists already, fitting it to your catalogue, your ERP and your pricing rules is a matter of weeks rather than a project starting from nothing.
